Build a detailed assessment tool for singles to assess marriage readiness profile with the detailed information below ensuring the assessment is professional and engaging to use using the guidelines below. The name is Singles Marriage Readiness Assessment Profile

Singles Marriage Readiness Assessment

Assessment Purpose

This assessment is designed to help singles explore the mindset, expectations, values, self-awareness, and relational capacities they are bringing toward marriage.

It does not determine whether someone is "worthy" of marriage or predict whether a future marriage will succeed.

It is a preparation tool.

The assessment asks:

"What are you bringing into marriage, and what may need intentional development before you build a life with another person?"

PART I — RESPONSE SCALE

Use a mixture of scenario, multiple-choice, and agreement questions.

For standard statements, use:

Strongly Disagree

Disagree

Neither Agree nor Disagree

Agree

Strongly Agree

Important: Do not display "correct" answers.

For scenario questions, randomize answer order where technically appropriate.

Some questions should be reverse-scored.

PART III — SCORING SYSTEM

AVOID A SINGLE "YOU ARE 83% READY" SCORE

Do not make the primary result:

"You are 83% ready for marriage."

This creates false precision.

Instead produce a Marriage Readiness Profile.

Each area receives a score from 0–100.

AREA SCORE

Convert Likert responses to:

1 = 0
2 = 25
3 = 50
4 = 75
5 = 100

Apply reverse scoring to designated questions.

Scenario questions should have weighted options based on the underlying construct.

READINESS CATEGORIES

80–100

STRONG FOUNDATION

"Your responses suggest a strong foundation in this area. You appear to have developed perspectives and attitudes that can support a healthy marriage."

60–79

DEVELOPING FOUNDATION

"You appear to have a good foundation in this area, while still having opportunities to develop greater clarity, intentionality, or practical preparation."

40–59

AREA TO EXPLORE

"Your responses suggest that this area deserves more intentional reflection before marriage."

0–39

PRIORITY PREPARATION

"Your responses suggest that this area may require significant attention and preparation. This does not mean you cannot have a successful marriage; it identifies an area where intentional growth could be particularly valuable."

PART V — CRITICAL BLIND-SPOT ENGINE

Do not simply calculate averages.

Create rules that compare responses across the assessment.

BLIND SPOT 1 — COMPROMISE

If:

Q77 indicates healthy compromise

BUT

Q55/Q59 indicate low willingness to change,

generate:

SOMETHING WORTH EXPLORING

"You value compromise in principle, but some of your responses suggest that adapting your own preferences may become difficult when the issue is personally important to you."

Reflection:

"What kinds of things would be hardest for you to compromise on?"

BLIND SPOT 2 — SPOUSE AS PERSONAL SOLUTION

If the user strongly agrees with statements suggesting:

"My spouse should make me happy."

"My spouse should strengthen my relationship with God."

"My spouse should help me overcome my personal weaknesses."

Generate:

YOUR EXPECTATION TO EXPLORE

"A spouse can support your growth, but marriage cannot carry responsibility for becoming the solution to every personal need."

BLIND SPOT 3 — FAMILY BOUNDARIES

If:

Q18/Q21/Q22 demonstrate strong family authority expectations,

generate:

A CONVERSATION YOU SHOULD HAVE BEFORE MARRIAGE

"What role should our families have in our decisions once we are married?"

BLIND SPOT 4 — MONEY & POWER

If the person strongly associates higher income with greater decision-making authority:

Generate:

FINANCIAL EXPECTATION TO EXPLORE

"How will you separate financial contribution from decision-making authority?"

BLIND SPOT 5 — CONFLICT

If the user:

Values conflict resolution

But chooses avoidance, retaliation, withdrawal, or dominance in scenarios

generate:

CONFLICT PATTERN TO EXPLORE

"You appear to value healthy conflict resolution, but some of your instinctive responses suggest that difficult conversations may be challenging when emotions are high."

BLIND SPOT 6 — SEXUAL EXPECTATIONS

If the user believes marriage should automatically resolve sexual or emotional needs:

Generate:

INTIMACY EXPECTATION TO EXPLORE

"Marriage can provide a context for intimacy, but it does not automatically resolve personal insecurity, communication difficulties, or differences in desire."

BLIND SPOT 7 — CHILDREN

If the user strongly expects children but has not considered differences in fertility, timing, parenting, or adoption:

Generate:

FAMILY PLANNING CONVERSATION

"Your desire for children appears important. Before marriage, explore what happens if the path to parenthood looks different from what you currently imagine."
